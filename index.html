    How the practice responds

    Two independent first-check successes on different variations at the current demand, including at least one numeric answer, unlock the next demand. Supported successes count as correct practice but do not, by themselves, unlock harder work. Earlier untested variations return between demanding questions. Once the available highest-demand families have been sampled, unseen related applications are preferred to repeating those same families.

    After a question is closed unsuccessfully, a related prerequisite and a later recheck are queued. Repeated difficulty lowers the working demand without deleting earlier achievements. There is no speed penalty and no learner-facing level display.

    “Independent” means first-check success without opening in-app help. It is not proof that no outside help was used. This is a local progress view, not an authenticated assessment record or a class dashboard.

    About this activity

    52 randomised variations covering the six skills in the original worksheet: conversions in both directions and addition, subtraction, multiplication and division. There are also comparisons, ordering, correcting notation, selecting data, ranges, unit changes, exact percentages, estimates and multi-step problems.

    All generated calculation values are positive, exact whole numbers or terminating decimals. Standard-form answers have a first factor of at most three decimal places and a power from −8 to 12, with extended powers reserved for later questions. Ordinary-number answers have no more than seven decimal places. This keeps arithmetic manageable; it is a classroom design choice, not a restriction on examination questions.

    Generated powers zero and one appear only in two multiple-choice checks. They are excluded from other questions, their standard-form answers, and their displayed worked methods, including different-number examples. This is a classroom selection rule: these powers are still mathematically valid in standard form and are not excluded by the examination specification.

    Standard-form answers must have the correct value and the required notation. A first factor from 1 to less than 10 and an integer power are required. Trailing zeros are accepted. Ordinary-number questions require an ordinary decimal, not calculator e-notation.

    No bulk answer-reveal control exists. Help gives methods and different-number examples. A maximum of three distinct, valid checks prevents endless answer guessing. A question must be closed before its solution is available in the final review; blank skipped questions never reveal a solution there.

    A standalone HTML file cannot provide tamper-proof examination security against developer tools, altered local storage or outside help. No passwords or answer-key obfuscation are presented as secure protection. Scoring here supports supervised learning, not high-stakes assessment.
