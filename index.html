Standard Form (Scientific Notation)

Need a hint? (click to expand)

Standard form means writing a number as a × 10n where 1 ≤ a < 10 and n is an integer.

Adding in standard form
  • Make the powers of 10 the same (rewrite one number if needed).
  • Add the coefficients and renormalise so 1 ≤ a < 10.
(3.2 × 105) + (7.1 × 105) = 10.3 × 105 = 1.03 × 106
Subtracting in standard form
  • Match powers of 10, subtract coefficients, renormalise.
(8.4 × 104) − (2.9 × 104) = 5.5 × 104
Multiplying in standard form
  • Multiply coefficients, add exponents, renormalise.
(6 × 103) × (2.5 × 104) = 15 × 107 = 1.5 × 108
Dividing in standard form
  • Divide coefficients, subtract exponents, renormalise.
(9 × 106) ÷ (3 × 102) = 3 × 104
Ordinary → Standard
  • Move the decimal so the first digit is non-zero; count moves for n.
0.00057 = 5.7 × 10−4     18,400,000 = 1.84 × 107
Standard → Ordinary
  • Shift the decimal point: right for positive n, left for negative n.
2.3 × 10−3 = 0.0023     7.5 × 104 = 75,000

Enter answers in standard form using the boxes: coefficient next to “×10” and the small power box on the top-right. For “Standard → Ordinary”, enter a normal number (you may use e-notation like 1.2e5).
